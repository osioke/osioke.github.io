Everything I've built & worked on

The longer version — projects, employment, education, and writing.

projects

  • LORE (2025–present): an organisational knowledge-transfer platform, built to capture what an experienced employee actually knows before they leave — from what they do rather than how they'd explain themselves if asked. Built and tested on Firebase and vanilla JavaScript. An early version paused after user research showed the HR/L&D workflow I'd assumed didn't match how these teams actually work. Back in active development since, after a proper diagnosis of what the knowledge-extraction pipeline was actually missing. Revisiting it this closely is also what sent me back to write out the fuller, nine-year history behind the underlying idea — I'd lost track of my own throughline and needed to see it laid out plainly again. Source at github.com/lore-platform/lore.
  • Devcenter & Gigson.co (2016–2017): grew a Lagos developer and designer community from 100 to over 2,500 members in a year, generating over $6,000 in revenue through community partnerships and sponsorships (including GitHub, Flutterwave, Stickermule, Ingressive, and Leadspace). While running it, I noticed people who joined asking beginner questions were, within months, answering hard ones for other members — with nobody managing that acceleration on purpose. I also conceived and led development of Gigson.co, a freelance talent marketplace that became the company's primary revenue product — it's still live today, though it's a Devcenter product now, no longer something I design or manage. I also led a distributed team building Disease Info (below).
  • Learn by Doing (2016–2017): the first deliberate attempt to build a system around that observation — combining real project work, tasks broken into manageable pieces, and one-on-one apprenticeship mentoring. Failed honestly and quickly: real enthusiasm at the start, a consistent drop-off after roughly two weeks, and incentives that didn't fix it.
  • Frintern (2018): a platform matching people to tech skill paths suited to their natural affinities, with a personalisation algorithm that hit over 90% accuracy across 1,000+ career matches, and a microlearning system delivered through WhatsApp and Slack. In a March–May 2018 pilot, participants learned a skill and applied it in 2–3 hours, down from a 20-hour baseline. Used to deliver paid training to African Fintech Foundry's Accelerator cohort and to Maliyo Games ahead of a product launch. Now sunsetted.
  • Owmi (2019): a chatbot-and-app platform connecting Lagos residents to trusted, treated water sources, built by a training cohort from real interviews with residents about a real problem. Fully prototyped and tested with real users; not continued, as it needed ongoing in-person operational work at a point when the direction had shifted toward digital products.
  • Growth Clinic & Community Staples (2019–2025): an open, distributed accelerator and incubator for communities, and its sister publication on the science of community management. Growth Clinic secured $10,000 in non-equity funding; Community Staples guided roughly 30 people through a custom training programme. Wound down at the end of 2025 — the model had drifted from what actually worked at Devcenter (placing people in real paid work, taking a share of the value) toward selling training directly to individuals.
  • Shop (2025): a full-stack inventory and sales-management system for a family convenience store — role-based access, real-time inventory tracking, credit management, and sales reporting. Built solo with Firebase.
  • What Is It Gameshow (Jul–Nov 2025, contract): an interactive gameshow application for a live streamer, built with Electron. Scoped, built, and delivered independently to brief as a paid engagement.
  • MangaTracker: a browser extension and companion web app for tracking manhwa, manga, manhua, and anime across any reading site, with Firebase-backed sync so a library stays consistent across devices. I built it to track my own manga list, and still use it that way.
  • Disease Info: a health-information progressive web app with an open API, built with a distributed team at Devcenter to make verified information on diseases in Nigeria accessible across media channels.
  • Gani: a digital museum documenting and exhibiting the culture around me. Currently a work in progress.

writing

  • Building Communities into Economies (2019) — open-access book distilling what watching real Lagos communities (a farmers' market, a taco restaurant, a dance studio) turn shared interest into shared economic value taught me about designing systems around that pattern.
  • From Knowledge to Judgement (2025) — the whitepaper underpinning LORE's approach to extracting tacit, hard-to-document expertise.
